Night of Paradise

1. 60 Days of Silence, Silent Flight

Seoul in 200X was no longer a city that never slept. It was a massive graveyard, and at the same time, a hunting ground for starving beasts. Sixty days ago, when an unknown virus struck the city center, people regarded it as a temporary disturbance. However, as blood-stained photos began appearing on Cyworld mini-homepages instead of "Today's Mood: Sadness," and only crackling noise was broadcast on TV instead of news, the world came to an end.

"Yeon-hee, adjust the gas. Lower it a little."

Seo-yoon whispered, her brown hair tied back tightly. She clutched a sharp, military dagger in her hand. Throwing weapons were neatly planted behind her back in preparation for emergencies.

"Alright. The wind is blowing west. Perfect."

Sitting in the basket cockpit, Yeon-hee adjusted the flame of the firebox. Her faint white hair fluttered in the night breeze, creating a mysterious atmosphere. The two girls hailed from a quiet tourist village rarely visited by outsiders. Thanks to this, they were able to survive the chaos of the early stages of the crisis. Although the nearby military unit succeeded in clearing out the zombies around their base, they were no match for the city center. With a severe shortage of ammunition and heavy weaponry, they chose to defend the outskirts rather than confront the sound-sensitive Legion.

However, Seoyun and Yeonhee had a hot air balloon. As long as they had gas, the sky was the safest road, and the rooftops of high-rise buildings were their stations. Every night, they silently cut through the sky to deliver relief supplies to survivors trapped at the tops of buildings or transport them to safety.

“Look over there. That’s the Paradise Hotel.”

Where Yeonhee pointed stood the ‘Paradise Hotel,’ boasting a majestic height of 100 stories. Miraculously, perhaps because the emergency generator was running, faint lights still lingered in a few spots on the upper floors of the building. However, there was absolutely no sign of human presence around the building. In the pitch-black darkness, that light was luring them in like the glow of fireflies.

2. Isolation on the 100th Floor and Farewell

The hot air balloon lowered its altitude over the hotel's rooftop helipad. The weight of the basket and the noise of the flames made a full landing too burdensome. Seoyun climbed onto the edge of the basket and looked down.

“Yeonhee, I’m going to jump from here. You need to get out of sight completely and wait at a safe altitude. You must not come this way until I give you the signal.”

“Okay. I’ll keep the radio on at all times. You need to call me immediately if anything happens. Be careful, Seoyun.”

As Yeonhee carefully adjusted the flames, the balloon approached close enough to almost touch the helipad floor. Seoyun lightly launched herself and landed on the rooftop floor. After confirming that she had landed safely, Yeonhee raised the flames again. The hot air balloon drifted away as if slowly being sucked into the darkness, and soon vanished into the night air.

Now, Seoyun was the only survivor left in this massive 100-story tomb. With the rainy season scheduled to begin in two weeks, they had to occupy this hotel—which was well-equipped with food and defensive infrastructure—as a fortress before then. Seoyun tightened her grip on the cold handle of a military dagger instead of her iriver MP3 player. When she opened the rooftop door, a hallway filled with the lingering chill of air conditioning appeared. The hotel was enveloped in silence. The carpeted hallway muffled the sound of footsteps, but that very silence only intensified the fear.

The penthouses on the 90th and 80th floors were spotless. It appeared that the guests had either already escaped by helicopter from the rooftop early in the crisis or met their end inside their rooms. Seoyun filled her bag with premium canned food, wine, and an emergency first-aid kit.

However, as she passed the 70th floor and descended to the 60th, the atmosphere changed. The walls were covered in dried bloodstains, and the doors were roughly broken.

3. The Shadow of the 50th Floor, The Appearance of a Mutant

The moment Seoyun descended the emergency stairs and opened the door to the 50th floor, she instinctively hid herself. The sound coming from the end of the hallway was not the typical ‘grumbling’ groan of a zombie. It was an unpleasant friction sound, like wet rubber dragging across the floor.

A figure crawling along the wall. It had abnormally long limbs, and its eyes had atrophied and were covered in smooth skin. Instead, its ears were abnormally developed. It was an auditory mutant.

‘Even if I had a gun, I wouldn’t be able to use it.’

Seoyun held her breath. The creature nodded its head and approached her. Seoyun took a small, silent rubber ball out of her pocket and threw it into the opposite hallway.

Tap, tap, tap…

The creature shot off like lightning to where the ball landed. Seizing the opportunity, Seoyun approached the creature from behind and plunged a military dagger deep into the vital spot at the back of its neck. It was a silent execution. However, from the 50th floor upwards, there were plenty of such men. The stairs were already blocked by obstacles, so Seoyun cautiously descended via the freight elevator shaft.

4. The Nightmare of the 3rd Floor Cafeteria

When Seoyun arrived at the grand banquet hall and buffet restaurant on the 3rd floor—a treasure trove of food—she hid herself under the overwhelming sense of intimidation. In the center of the spacious hall, capable of accommodating thousands, lay a predator of bizarre form rather than a mere pile of corpses.

It was a giant mutant zombie, resembling dozens of zombies grotesquely intertwined to merge into the shape of a massive centipede or a polypod. It was a monster that had grown in size by devouring the bodies of countless guests who had taken refuge in the cafeteria, as well as the surrounding zombies. Dozens of limbs were clustered on either side of its torso like thick bundles of muscle, planted firmly on the ground; this form possessed powerful mobility, capable of sprinting at madness to pounce on Seoyun the moment she spotted her. Countless heads sprouted from every corner of its body, each letting out hungry groans.

To reach the food storage, she had to pass by that creature. Seoyun spotted the large gas cylinders in the dining hall kitchen.

‘The rule is to remain silent, but I can’t help it with this one.’

Seoyun opened all the gas valves. Then, lowering the shutter near the dining hall entrance halfway, she used a lighter from a distance to make makeshift Molotov cocktails.

Catching her rapid breath, Seoyun lit the cloth at the tip of the Molotov cocktail. As an orange flame flickered in the darkness, the giant monster’s countless mouths let out grotesque groans in unison toward the light.

Seoyun threw the Molotov cocktail. The flames spread toward the center of the gas-filled hall. Boom!

The entire hotel shook with a tremendous explosion. The giant monster zombie burned to ashes in the flames, screaming. The shock of the explosion caused the horde of zombies gathered nearby to collapse helplessly. Seoyun was thrown back by the impact, but she regained her senses and ran straight to the food storage. Large quantities of food ingredients with long expiration dates were piled high.

5. The Seal of the First Floor and the New Fortress

Hearing the explosion, the zombies outside the hotel began to panic. Zombies on the nearby streets slowly turned toward the direction of the sound. Yeonhee, watching from high above, sent a radio message.

“Seoyun! I can see it from my position! They are swarming in pitch black toward the entrance on the first floor! We have to stop them quickly!”

Seoyun ran from the third floor to the railing overlooking the first-floor lobby. Shadows of zombies illuminated by the streetlights were pouring into the lobby.

Seoyun pressed the forced-lower switch for the fire shutter she had prepared in advance. However, perhaps due to the shock of the explosion, the shutter stopped midway, emitting an unpleasant mechanical sound. Seoyun frantically tore off the control panel, but she had no way of knowing which of the intricately tangled wires inside was the descent signal. Instead of panicking, she looked at the massive chandelier hanging from the lobby ceiling.

She jumped from the railing, grabbed the chandelier cord, and used the momentum to launch herself toward a manual control lever installed high on the wall. Instead of touching the wires, she spotted a red-painted emergency manual opening/closing lever. Seoyun shattered the reinforced glass cover holding the lever in place with the heavy handle of her military dagger in a single stroke, and pulled the lever down roughly with her entire body weight.

Rattling, bang!

The steel shutter descended heavily to the floor, surrendering to gravity, completely blocking the zombies' entry. The hotel had now become a massive fortress, cut off from the outside world. Gasping for breath, Seoyun picked up the radio. “Situation over. Yeon-hee, you can come down now. Come to the rooftop helipad.”

6. A Feast at Paradise

Two weeks later, as promised, heavy rain began to pour down from the sky. However, the Sky Lounge on the 100th floor of the Paradise Hotel remained perfectly peaceful.

“Wow, this is seriously amazing. We couldn't even dream of this in our village.”

Yeon-hee marveled as she took a bite of a steak made from premium canned beef. The table was filled with various food ingredients brought from the third-floor warehouse, champagne, and luxurious items.

Seoyun looked out the window. In the pouring rain, the zombies on the ground were wandering, having lost their way. Now that the hotel’s interior was cleaned up, this place was, true to its name, a paradise of their own. The emergency generator supplied power reliably, and there was plenty of beverages as well as water.

“Yeon-hee, we can hold out here for quite a while now.”

“I know. Once the rain stops, let’s take the hot air balloon back and bring the others over.” Seoyun nodded at Yeonhee's words. She took out the old horizontal flip phone she had kept in her pocket for sixty days. The signal was dead, but inside, happy memories from before the disaster remained as photographs.

The brown-haired girl raised her champagne glass. Although daily life in the 2000s had collapsed, they had found a stronger friendship and a code of survival amidst the ruins. Even in the torrential rain of the night sky, the lights on the top floor of the Paradise Hotel shone quietly like an unextinguishable lighthouse. It was a confident declaration of survival from the two girls to a ruined world.
